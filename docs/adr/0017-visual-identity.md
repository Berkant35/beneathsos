# 0017. Visual identity: palette, typeface, logo, voice and accessibility targets

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-11

## Context

> A very usable, readable interface for people at a collapsed building in an emergency.

The primary user is an untrained volunteer under stress, outdoors (sun, dust, night), using one hand,
possibly with gloves. The victim-mode user is trapped, in the dark, with a draining battery. The brand
also speaks to AFAD, NGOs, contributors and supporters: it must look trustworthy, not like a hobby
project. The decisions below were made one by one with the maintainer, with options shown side by
side and contrast ratios measured.

## Decision

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Feeling | Calm body + high-visibility accent for actions and attention |
| Theme | Follow the phone's light/dark setting everywhere — except victim mode, which is always dark (amended 2026-10-13: its screen stays on for hours on a trapped phone's battery) |
| Typeface | Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (variable 200–800, SIL OFL 1.1); tabular figures for changing numbers |
| Icons | Material Symbols Rounded + 5–6 custom domain icons on the logo's grid and stroke |
| Palette | Ink navy `#1B2A4E` / `#9DB2E0`, safety orange `#FF7900` / `#FF8C2E` with dark text `#1A0D00`, error red `#B3261E` / `#FFB4AB` |
| Logo | "Signal exit": straight rubble line, phone dot below, three arcs rising and fading |
| Voice | Short, calm, directive; formal "siz" in Turkish; imperative in English |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA + field ergonomics (≥ 56 px targets, thumb zone, 200 % text, haptics + sound, reduce motion, never colour alone) |
| Source of truth | Code first: brand assets generated by `tools/brand`, tokens in `beneathsos_ui` (phase 1.2) |

Measured contrast: white on navy 14.1; dark text on orange 7.3 (light) / 8.2 (dark); error red on
white 6.5; dark-theme primary 8.9; body text 16.1 (light) / 15.9 (dark).

## Alternatives

- **Palette A — deep teal + signal amber.** Calm, distinctive, no resemblance to government apps.
  Rejected in favour of B, which reads more like rescue and first-aid equipment.
- **Palette C — graphite + high-vis lime.** Highest contrast; felt cold and game-like for volunteers.
- **Logo A — wordmark only.** Most legible, but the app icon would be just "SOS" text.
- **Logo C — probability rings.** Represents the output, but reads as a target or crosshair, which
  contradicts "never claim a location".
- **Logo B with a fractured rubble line.** More literal, but the breaks disappear at small sizes.
- **System fonts / Inter.** No brand identity, or a typeface seen everywhere; Atkinson was designed
  for legibility in poor conditions.
- **Figma as source of truth.** Familiar to designers, but adds a sync burden and the connector was
  not set up; code-first keeps one source reviewed in pull requests.

## Consequences

- **Navy and orange are also AFAD's colours.** Mitigations: deliberately different tones (ink navy,
  ANSI safety orange), our own logo and typeface, and the non-affiliation statement in onboarding,
  About and store listings (`docs/brand/README.md`).
- White text on orange is forbidden (2.6); orange always carries dark text.
- Orange text or icons on the light background are forbidden too (2.4, below the 3:1 non-text
  minimum of WCAG 1.4.11): in light theme orange is a fill only. In dark theme they are fine (8.1).
- The Red Cross / Red Crescent emblems are protected by law and are never used; first-aid character
  comes from colour and form instead.
- Outer arcs are drawn with opacity, so on navy they appear brownish; acceptable as approved, can be
  revisited with solid lighter tones.
- The iOS dark app icon has a transparent background, not the `#0C111D` of the spec: iOS draws its
  own dark background behind dark-appearance icons, so the symbol matches the system look.
- The font adds about 115 KB to the app (phase 1.2).
- Every brand asset is reproducible: `tools/brand/build.py`, covered by tests for the contrast table,
  logo geometry, 16 px legibility and the iOS tinted / Android monochrome icon layers.
